# Mafia · Arcade house rules

Five to ten players join a private room, each on their own device. The host participates and moderates phase transitions. At 5–6 players there is one Mafia; at 7–10 there are two. Exactly one Doctor and one Detective are town; everyone else is ordinary town. Roles are shuffled anew each round.

Night comes first. Each living Mafia member privately selects a living player other than themself. If both Mafia select the same player, that player is attacked; if they disagree, nobody is attacked. The Doctor privately protects any living player, including themself, and prevents an attack on that player. The Detective privately investigates one other living player and receives “Mafia” or “not Mafia” in their own case file. Results are not broadcast or automatically announced. When all night actions arrive the host reveals dawn.

During the day, everyone may discuss aloud or over their own voice call. The host opens voting; every living player privately votes for another living player or abstains. The unique highest vote-getter is eliminated. A tie for highest, or no votes, eliminates nobody. The host counts ballots after everyone acts. Eliminated players can watch but may not act or vote; roles stay secret until the game ends. Night and day alternate. Town wins as soon as every Mafia member is eliminated. Mafia wins when living Mafia equal or outnumber living town. The host can explicitly mark a **disconnected** player absent for the current phase, counting their night action as no action or their day ballot as an abstention; reconnecting restores their seat in the next phase. An absent Mafia action can mean no night attack. The host must be online to resolve phases; participants should keep their tabs open.

This is an explicit **house variant**, not a claim that all Mafia groups play alike. The public [Mafia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_(party_game)) describes the informed minority, alternating private night and public day, investigative and protective roles, and town elimination/Mafia parity goals. [Andrew Plotkin’s Werewolf rules](https://www.eblong.com/zarf/werewolf.html) give another public account of alternating phases and player elimination. Our simultaneous individual Mafia choices, tie/no-elimination rules, role counts, abstentions, and disconnected-seat procedure are Arcade-specific decisions.

**Privacy boundary:** The host device stores the complete authoritative game, including every role, target and ballot, in its room recovery storage. The host is a *trusted peer*, not a cryptographically blind moderator. Other devices receive only their own role, Mafia allies if applicable, their own investigation results and their own submitted choice, plus public progress counts and outcomes. Room signaling uses shared invitation/answer URLs; keep those links private. The game has no in-app chat; use voice or talk in person.
